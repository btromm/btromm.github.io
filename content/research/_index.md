My PhD asks how brains and machines build **cognitive maps**: internal models of the world that support flexible behaviour. I'm especially interested in grid cells, and in why such codes take the form they do.

## which architectures give rise to grid codes?

I use reinforcement learning and successor-representation models to work out which architectures, objectives and policies give rise to grid-like codes, and which of these ingredients are necessary rather than just sufficient.

## before the PhD

I was a research engineer in Nikolas Karalis's neuronal circuits and brain dynamics group at the Paris Brain Institute. At Oxford and Maastricht I studied how functional hierarchy in the brain changes under psychedelics. Before that, I modelled homeostatic regulation of ion channels in Eve Marder's lab at Brandeis.
